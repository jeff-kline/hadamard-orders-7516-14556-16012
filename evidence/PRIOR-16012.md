# Earlier constructions at orders 16012 and 64012

The November 24 weighing-matrix result already implies both orders. In its binary Golay corollary take respectively length 1000 or 4000. Then 4g+1 is the prime 4001 or 16001, and the real Hadamard order is 4(4g+3). Priority relative to earlier literature remains unresolved at both orders. [Released corollary](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24519197)

An older construction gives **32024 and 128024**. In Craigen, Holzmann and Kharaghani's Corollary 16 take binary Golay length 4000 or 16000 and complex Golay length 3. The resulting order is 8(4000+3) or 8(16000+3). The binary lengths are respectively 2²·10³ and 2⁴·10³. These are explicit historical benchmarks at the two odd parts. [Craigen, Holzmann and Kharaghani](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(01)00162-5)

| Yamada, Theorem 5 | Prime 4001 requires a symmetric real conference matrix of order 2002. Its parameter 2001 = 3·23·29 fails the sum-of-two-squares condition. | Prime 16001 requires a symmetric real conference matrix of order 8002. Its parameter 8001 = 3²·7·127 also fails that condition. |
| Cooper–Wallis | The odd part 4003 is prime. A T-system or Williamson-type system at 4003 would suffice. | 16003 = 13·1231. A T-system at one factor and a Williamson-type system at the other would suffice. Yamada actually lists an H(4·1231) on p. 378, from Theorem 4. Her output is a bordered Hadamard array. Applying Cooper–Wallis still requires the structured input. |
| Classical binary Legendre-pair series | Pair length 8005 has factors 5 and 1601. The Szekeres field candidate is 16011. | Pair length 32005 has three distinct prime factors. The Szekeres field candidate is 64011. |

The odd-exponent prime factors 3 modulo 4 exclude the real conference matrices in the second row. The field and factor tests in the last row cover Szekeres and the twin-prime construction. Other methods of producing the required pairs remain possible. ## Historical sources with incomplete coverage

For odd part 4003 the Handbook entry is the **second character** in the 4000-column, 00-row group of Table 1.53 on printed p. 278. A dot there would establish previously recorded existence. The public text layer gave the caption without the cells in the earlier check. The publisher preview, the editor’s update and the search for a locally archived chapter yielded no readable copy of that cell. Odd part 16003 exceeds the table's range. [Handbook preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781420010541_A25103714/preview-9781420010541_A25103714.pdf) · [Printed p. 278](https://archive.org/details/handbookofcombin0000unse/page/278/mode/1up) · [Editor’s updates](https://site.uvm.edu/jdinitz/?page_id=404)

Lou, Xia and Xia's July 2019 abstract describes two special cases for prime lengths 6m+1. Both 4003 = 6·667+1 = 56²+3·17² and 1231 = 6·205+1 satisfy the prime congruence. For 1231 the integral representation is 34²+3·5². The publisher download registered with Crossref returned HTML rather than the paper. The full hypotheses of the two cases were unavailable in the publisher search and author listing. If the theorem supplies T-matrices at 4003, then Cooper–Wallis gives order 16012 directly. If it supplies them at 1231, a Williamson system at 13 completes the route to 64012 (Turyn's prime power 2·13−1 = 25). The abstract alone does not settle either application.
